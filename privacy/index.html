Privacy Policy

Last updated 27 September 2026 · App: FitMax · iOS bundle: com.fitmax.sameh · Android package: com.fitmax.sameh

FitMax is published on the App Store by Samet Taha Öndeş and is also available on Google Play. This policy covers both the iPhone and the Android app; “we” means the publisher of the version you installed. There are no accounts. Your profile, your photos, your reports and your training log stay on your phone. Photos leave the phone only when you ask for an analysis, and this page says exactly where they go and what happens to them.

What leaves your phone

DataWhere it goes, and why
The body photos you take or pick for an analysisSent to FitMax's own relay server, hosted by Vercel, and forwarded to Anthropic, the processing service that produces the analysis. Anthropic processes them to provide the service, under its own commercial terms; FitMax does not use them for advertising. Our server passes them through and does not store them. Before the app sends anything to Anthropic for the first time, it asks for your permission.
Your profile — age, sex, height, weight, goal, training history — and the measurements the phone took from the photosSent with the photos so the scores have context. For a training programme, the same profile and the scores of your latest report go to Anthropic the same way, together with the answers you gave about days, equipment and goal. Your name is included in a programme request only so the programme can address you.
An anonymous purchase identifier, your purchase history and subscription status, and purchase events such as loading the offer, buying and restoringHeld by RevenueCat, our subscription service, and by the app store, so the app knows whether your subscription is active and we can see how the subscription is used overall. Because there is no account, none of it is tied to a name or an email address. Payment details are handled by the store — Apple on iPhone, Google Play on Android — and never reach us.
Network metadata — IP address, request timing, error classProcessed by Vercel, which hosts the relay server, to run and protect the service. Requests carry no user identifier: nothing on the server can tie an analysis to you.
Requests for exercise animations and the exercise catalogueLoaded from public content servers that host them. The requests carry nothing about you; like any web server, those servers see your IP address.
A content report, only if you send oneSent to a Google Form operated by FitMax. It contains no photo and no profile data; see Content reports.
Android only: diagnostics from Google's ML Kit and an installation identifierML Kit is the library the Android app uses to detect your pose on the phone. It sends Google device and app details, performance metrics, configuration and error codes, together with an identifier for this installation of the app, which Google uses to maintain and improve ML Kit. They contain no photo, no body measurement and no profile data, and they do not reach us.

Everything in this table travels over encrypted HTTPS connections; the app makes no unencrypted requests.

Location, contacts and identity are never collected. There are no ads, no account, no advertising SDK, no analytics profile and no tracking, and the app does not use an advertising identifier on either platform. On iPhone, the skeleton measurements, the face crop for your avatar and the placement of scores on your photo all run on the phone with Apple's on-device vision framework; nothing leaves for that. On Android the same work runs on the phone with Google's ML Kit, and no photo leaves for it either; see Android (Google Play).

Content reports

If an analysis or a programme looks wrong, offensive or harmful, you can report it from inside the app. The report contains the reason you choose, a note if you type one, whether it concerns a report or a programme together with that item's internal reference in the app, the app version, the platform and the app's language. It contains no photo, no profile data and no device identifier. It is sent to a Google Form operated by FitMax and used only to review the reported content.

What stays on your phone

Your name and profile; the three photos of every analysis and its report; your programmes, workout logs, personal records and favourites; your language and timer settings. On iPhone the photos are kept in the app's private storage, excluded from iCloud backup and encrypted while the device is locked. On Android the app keeps all of it in its private storage and takes no part in Google's cloud backup — see Android (Google Play). Uninstalling the app removes all of it.

Nothing is sold. Nothing goes to advertisers. No profile is built about you, and your photos are never used to train anything of ours.

Android (Google Play)

The Android app is available on Google Play under the package name com.fitmax.sameh. It collects and sends exactly what the table above lists. Its privacy practices differ from the iPhone app only in these points:

  • Pose detection with Google's ML Kit. The skeleton measurements, the face crop for your avatar and the placement of scores on your photo are computed on the phone by Google's ML Kit instead of Apple's vision framework. No photo leaves the phone for this. ML Kit sends Google the diagnostics and the installation identifier listed in the table above.
  • No cloud backup. The app opts out of Android backup, so nothing it stores is copied to your Google account. Your photos and reports are also left out of device-to-device transfer when you move to a new phone. Uninstalling the app removes everything it stored.
  • Payments through Google Play. Subscriptions are bought, billed and renewed through Google Play's billing system. Google handles your payment details; we never see them. RevenueCat, the subscription service, receives the purchase record from Google Play together with the anonymous purchase identifier.
  • Permissions. Camera, only when you take a photo in the app; internet and network status, to send an analysis and to notice when you are offline. Photos from the gallery are chosen with Android's photo picker, so the app sees only the photos you pick, never your whole library. On Android 8 and 9 the app also asks for storage access, only when you save a report card to your gallery.
  • No advertising ID. The app does not use the Android advertising ID, shows no ads and does not track you across other apps or websites.

How long it is kept

On your phone: until you delete it or uninstall the app. On our server: photos and profile data are not retained once the reply is sent — there is no cache and no file under your name. Anthropic and Vercel handle what they receive under their own terms and data-retention policies. RevenueCat keeps the anonymous purchase identifier and purchase history while they are needed to run your subscription and meet accounting duties, or until you ask us to delete them. Apple and Google keep store purchase records, and on Android Google keeps ML Kit diagnostics, under their own policies.

Your rights

You can see everything the app holds inside the app, change your profile in Settings, and delete it all — see Delete your data. You can withdraw the permission to send your photos and profile details to Anthropic at any time in Settings; the next analysis will ask for it again. If you are in Türkiye (KVKK) or the EEA (GDPR) these rights are yours by law; we apply them to everyone wherever they live. Write to mehmetsancaktutannn3@gmail.com, or use Give Feedback in Settings if your request concerns a purchase — it attaches the anonymous purchase identifier for you. The same rights and the same steps apply to the iPhone and the Android app.

Children

FitMax is for adults. It is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect anything from them. Body photos of anyone under 18 must not be submitted. On both the App Store and Google Play, FitMax is meant only for people aged 18 and over.

Not a medical service

FitMax is not a medical device. Its scores and its body-fat figure are estimates from photographs, not measurements or diagnoses, and nothing in a report is medical advice. Ask a doctor before changing your diet or training because of a number.

Changes

If this policy changes materially the date at the top changes with it, and the app points here again.
